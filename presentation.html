Part 1: Classical Machine Learning

Classical Support Vector Machine: The Maximum Margin Principle

Supervised binary classification grounded in Vapnik-Chervonenkis structural risk minimization.

Geometric Margin & Support Vectors

  • Decision Hyperplane: Separates classes using $\mathbf{w} \cdot \mathbf{x} + b = 0$.
  • Margin Maximization: Maximizes the geometric margin $\frac{2}{\|\mathbf{w}\|}$ between opposing support classes, mathematically minimizing generalization error bounds.
  • Support Vectors: Points satisfying $y_i(\mathbf{w} \cdot \mathbf{x}_i + b) = 1$. Only boundary points dictate the decision plane; all interior points can be safely discarded.
  • Soft-Margin Formulation: Slack variables $\xi_i \ge 0$ allow controlled violations on noisy real-world data, balanced by regularization penalty $C$.
Primal Soft-Margin Objective
$$\min_{\mathbf{w}, b, \mathbf{\xi}} \;\; \frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|^2 + C \sum_{i=1}^M \xi_i \quad \text{s.t.} \quad y_i(\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{x}_i + b) \ge 1 - \xi_i, \;\; \xi_i \ge 0$$
Balances maximizing margin width ($\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|^2$) with minimizing training margin violations ($\sum \xi_i$).

Lagrangian Wolfe Dual Formulation

Using Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions, we convert the primal problem into a convex quadratic dual:

Lagrangian Wolfe Dual Problem
$$\max_{\mathbf{\alpha}} \;\; \sum_{i=1}^M \alpha_i - \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^M \alpha_i \alpha_j y_i y_j (\mathbf{x}_i \cdot \mathbf{x}_j) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad 0 \le \alpha_i \le C, \;\; \sum_{i=1}^M \alpha_i y_i = 0$$
$\alpha_i$ are the Lagrange multipliers. $\alpha_i > 0$ denotes active support vectors.
Optimal Decision Function
$$f(\mathbf{x}) = \mathrm{sign}\left( \sum_{i=1}^M \alpha_i y_i (\mathbf{x}_i \cdot \mathbf{x}) + b \right)$$
Crucial Insight: Data enters both training and inference strictly via inner products $(\mathbf{x}_i \cdot \mathbf{x})$!
Part 1: Classical Machine Learning

The Kernel Trick: Overcoming Non-Linearity

Operating implicitly in high-dimensional feature spaces without computing coordinates.

The Kernel Principle

  • Real-World Non-Linearity: Financial market trends and price reversals are non-linearly distributed in Euclidean input space $\mathbb{R}^d$.
  • Cover's Theorem: Casting complex data into a higher-dimensional space $\mathcal{H}$ via non-linear mapping $\phi(\mathbf{x})$ substantially increases the probability of linear separability.
  • The 'Trick' (Implicit Evaluation): Explicit computation of $\phi(\mathbf{x})$ is computationally intractable. We replace the inner product $\langle \phi(\mathbf{x}), \phi(\mathbf{x}') \rangle$ with a direct kernel function $K(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}')$!
Mercer's Condition & Inner Product Mapping
$$K(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}') = \langle \phi(\mathbf{x}), \phi(\mathbf{x}') \rangle_{\mathcal{H}}$$
Any symmetric function yielding a positive semi-definite (PSD) Gram matrix guarantees a valid reproducing kernel Hilbert space.

Standard Classical Kernels & Bottlenecks

  • Linear Kernel: $K(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}') = \mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{x}'$ (standard dot product in input space).
  • Polynomial Kernel: $K(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}') = (\mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{x}' + c)^d$ (cross-feature interactions up to degree $d$).
Radial Basis Function (RBF Gaussian Kernel)
$$K_{\mathrm{RBF}}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}') = \exp\left(-\gamma \|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}'\|^2\right)$$
Implicitly maps to infinite-dimensional Hilbert space based purely on Euclidean geometric distance.
The Fundamental Classical Bottleneck
Classical kernels treat feature space as local metric distance balls. Can quantum superposition and entanglement establish richer non-linear correlations?
Part 2: The Quantum Transition

The Quantum Leap: Why Combine Quantum & SVM?

The natural mathematical synergy between quantum state overlaps and kernel methods (Schuld & Killoran, 2019).

1. Natural Inner Product Evaluator

SVM relies strictly on evaluating inner products $K(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j)$. Quantum hardware cannot easily output large state vectors without tomography, but it natively measures quantum state overlaps (transition fidelity $|\langle \psi(\mathbf{x}_i) | \psi(\mathbf{x}_j) \rangle|^2$) through wave interference!

2. Exponential Hilbert Space Scaling

An $n$-qubit register lives in a $2^n$-dimensional complex Hilbert space $\mathcal{H} \cong \mathbb{C}^{2^n}$:

  • 4 Qubits: $2^4 = 16$ dimensions
  • 20 Qubits: $2^{20} = 1,048,576$ dimensions
  • 50 Qubits: $2^{50} \approx 1.12 \times 10^{15}$ dimensions (exceeds classical supercomputer RAM!)

3. Classically Intractable Feature Maps

Havlíček et al. (Nature, 2019) proved that quantum feature maps using non-commuting Pauli-Z entangling gates produce kernel matrices that are provably hard to estimate or sample classically.

4. Guaranteed Global Convex Optimization

Variational Quantum Classifiers (VQC) suffer from severe barren plateaus (vanishing gradients in parameterized circuits). QSVM completely circumvents this:

Quantum hardware computes the kernel matrix $\implies$ A classical quadratic programming (QP) solver guarantees the unique global optimum!

Part 2: Quantum Data Encoding

Quantum Feature Maps: Encoding Classical Data into Qubits

Unitary transformation $U_\Phi(\mathbf{x})$ mapping classical feature vectors $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ into quantum statevectors $|\psi(\mathbf{x})\rangle$.

Quantum State Preparation & Fidelity

Quantum State Representation
$$|\psi(\mathbf{x})\rangle = U_\Phi(\mathbf{x}) |0\rangle^{\otimes n} \in \mathcal{H} \cong \mathbb{C}^{2^n}$$
Encodes real-valued vector $\mathbf{x}$ as rotation angles inside a $2^n$-dimensional Hilbert space.
Quantum Transition Fidelity (Kernel Value)
$$K(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j) = |\langle \psi(\mathbf{x}_i) | \psi(\mathbf{x}_j) \rangle|^2 = |\langle 0^{\otimes n} | U_\Phi^\dagger(\mathbf{x}_j) U_\Phi(\mathbf{x}_i) | 0^{\otimes n} \rangle|^2$$
• Strictly bounded: $0 \le K(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j) \le 1.0$
• $K(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_i) = 1.0$ (identical vectors); $K(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j) = 0.0$ (orthogonal states)
• Symmetric and positive semi-definite (PSD) $\implies$ Valid Mercer Kernel!

Second-Order Pauli-Z (ZZFeatureMap) Architecture

Parameterized unitary circuit $U_\Phi(\mathbf{x}) = \left[ U_Z(\mathbf{x}) H^{\otimes n} \right]^2$ with depth $t=2$:

1. Single-Qubit Phase Rotations
$$\exp\left( i \sum_{i=1}^n x_i Z_i \right) \implies \text{Encodes individual feature magnitudes}$$
2. Pairwise Two-Qubit Entangling Rotations
$$\exp\left( i \sum_{i < j} 2(\pi - x_i)(\pi - x_j) Z_i Z_j \right)$$
Entangles pairs of qubits to capture non-linear cross-feature correlations.
Part 3: QSVM Paradigms

Two Principal Formulations of QSVM in Literature

Comparing the fault-tolerant HHL linear-algebraic approach vs the NISQ-ready hybrid kernel estimation.

Fault-Tolerant Formulation

HHL Least-Squares QSVM (Rebentrost et al. 2014)

Least-Squares Linear System Formulation
$$\begin{bmatrix} 0 & \mathbf{1}^T \\ \mathbf{1} & K + \gamma^{-1} I \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} b \\ \mathbf{\alpha} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \mathbf{y} \end{bmatrix} \implies A\mathbf{z} = \mathbf{b}$$
Solves matrix inversion $A^{-1}\mathbf{b}$ on quantum hardware in $O(\log(N \cdot d))$ runtime.
  • Exponential Speedup: $O(\log N)$ scaling vs classical $O(N^3)$.
  • Severe NISQ Bottleneck: Requires QRAM (quantum RAM) to load classical data into quantum states in $O(\log N)$, and deep phase estimation circuits requiring fault-tolerant error correction.
  • State Output: Result is encoded in quantum state $|\alpha, b\rangle$; extracting values requires quantum tomography.
NISQ-Ready Formulation (Implemented in Our Project)

Hybrid Quantum Kernel Estimation (Havlíček et al. 2019)

Quantum Kernel Evaluation Pipeline
$$K_{ij} = |\langle 0^{\otimes n} | U_\Phi^\dagger(\mathbf{x}_j) U_\Phi(\mathbf{x}_i) | 0^{\otimes n} \rangle|^2 \implies \text{Classical Dual QP Solver}$$
Quantum computer evaluates kernel matrix; classical computer solves convex quadratic dual problem.
  • No QRAM Required: Operates directly on classical input feeds.
  • Shallow Circuit Depth: Can execute on noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) processors without error correction.
  • Global Optimum: Preserves classical SVM's guaranteed convex convergence without barren plateaus.
Part 3: QSVM Circuit Architecture

The Compute-Uncompute Kernel Estimation Circuit

Direct state overlap measurement via inversion testing without requiring extra ancilla qubits.

Inversion Test Transition Probability Equation
$$P(|0000\rangle) = |\langle 0000 | U_\Phi^\dagger(\mathbf{x}_j) U_\Phi(\mathbf{x}_i) | 0000 \rangle|^2 = |\langle \psi(\mathbf{x}_j) | \psi(\mathbf{x}_i) \rangle|^2 = K(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j)$$
The probability of all qubits collapsing back to ground state $|0000\rangle$ equals the exact kernel fidelity!

Step-by-Step Circuit Flow

  • 1. Initialize: Prepare 4-qubit register in ground state $|0000\rangle$.
  • 2. Encode $\mathbf{x}_i$: Apply parameterized unitary feature map: $|\psi(\mathbf{x}_i)\rangle = U_\Phi(\mathbf{x}_i)|0000\rangle$.
  • 3. Uncompute $\mathbf{x}_j$: Apply Hermitian adjoint $U_\Phi^\dagger(\mathbf{x}_j)$ in reverse gate order with inverted rotation angles $(-\theta)$.
  • 4. Readout: Measure in computational $Z$-basis. Count frequency of bitstring 0000.

Why Compute-Uncompute Beats SWAP-Test

  • Zero Ancilla Qubits: The SWAP test requires an auxiliary ancilla qubit and controlled-SWAP (Fredkin) gates, which are very noisy to synthesize.
  • Half the Hardware Complexity: Compute-uncompute requires only the original $n$ qubits.
  • Ideal for NISQ Hardware: Minimizes gate error accumulation across physical superconducting qubits.

Part 4: Data Preprocessing

Feature Engineering & The Crucial $[0, \pi]$ Quantum Scaling

Extracting 4 technical indicators and mapping them cleanly to quantum rotation angles.

4 Core Technical Financial Features

  • Feature 1: Relative Strength Index (RSI 14-Day)
    Measures velocity and magnitude of directional price movements on a scale of 0 to 100. Overbought ($>70$), oversold ($<30$).
  • Feature 2: MACD Normalized (Moving Average Convergence Divergence)
    $\mathrm{EMA}_{12} - \mathrm{EMA}_{26}$, normalized by 9-day signal EMA to allow cross-asset comparability.
  • Feature 3: Moving Average Ratio ($\mathrm{SMA}_{10} / \mathrm{SMA}_{50}$)
    Ratio of short-term trend to medium-term trend. Captures bullish golden cross ($>1.0$) and bearish death cross ($<1.0$).
  • Feature 4: Normalized Volume Change ($\Delta\mathrm{Volume}$)
    Percentage change in volume relative to 20-day average volume. Confirms breakout momentum.

The Mathematical Reason for $[0, \pi]$ Scaling

Feature Vector Scaling Transformation
$$\mathbf{x} = [x_{\mathrm{RSI}}, \; x_{\mathrm{MACD}}, \; x_{\mathrm{MARatio}}, \; x_{\mathrm{Vol}}]^T \in [0, \pi]^4$$
Scaler is fit strictly on the 250 training days and applied to out-of-sample test days without lookahead bias.
  • $2\pi$-Periodicity Problem: Single-qubit phase rotations $R_z(\theta) = \exp(-i \frac{\theta}{2} Z)$ are $2\pi$-periodic. Rotation angles beyond $2\pi$ wrap around and produce identical quantum states!
  • Two-Qubit Phase Explosion: In the ZZ entangling gate, the angle is $\phi_{ij}(\mathbf{x}) = 2(\pi - x_i)(\pi - x_j)$. If features spanned $[0, 2\pi]$, angles would wrap uncontrollably up to $8\pi^2$!
  • Bijective Hilbert Mapping: Restricting each feature to $[0, \pi]$ guarantees a unique, one-to-one mapping into Hilbert space.
Part 5: Quantum Circuit Implementation

4-Qubit Circuit Architecture for Stock Prediction

Gate construction mapping 4 technical indicators into a 16-dimensional complex Hilbert space.

Circuit Architecture & Entanglement

Second-Order ZZ Feature Map Formulation
$$U_\Phi(\mathbf{x}) = \left[ U_Z(\mathbf{x}) H^{\otimes 4} \right]^2 \quad \text{with} \quad \phi_{ij}(\mathbf{x}) = 2(\pi - x_i)(\pi - x_j)$$
Entangling coupling is synthesized using physical elementary gates: $\mathrm{CNOT} \to R_z(\phi_{ij}) \to \mathrm{CNOT}$.
  • Linear Nearest-Neighbor Connectivity: Entangles adjacent pairs $(q_0, q_1)$, $(q_1, q_2)$, $(q_2, q_3)$. Matches physical superconducting hardware layout without SWAP gate overhead.
  • Circuit Depth: 16 gates per repetition $\times$ 2 reps = 32 physical gates.

Qubit to Indicator Mapping

Part 5: Engineering Innovation

Simulation Optimization: Achieving a 700× Speedup

Replacing naive sequential circuit sampling with exact vectorized statevector Hermitian inner products.

The Naive Sequential Simulation Bottleneck

  • Combinatorial Explosion: To evaluate the Gram matrix:
    • Training pairs: $250 \times 249 / 2 = 31,125$ circuits
    • Testing pairs: $100 \times 250 = 25,000$ circuits
    • Total individual quantum circuits: 56,125!
  • The 5-Minute Gateway Timeout Failure: Simulating 56,125 circuits sequentially via Qiskit's ComputeUncompute took $> 300$ seconds, causing web servers to crash with 504 timeouts!
  • Shot Noise Fluctuations: Sampling with 1,024 shots produces statistical binomial noise ($\sim 3.1\%$), making the Gram matrix non-positive semi-definite (breaking Mercer's theorem).

Our Vectorized BLAS Solution (0.42s Runtime)

Vectorized Statevector Kernel Formulation
$$K_{ij} = |\mathbf{\Psi}_i^\dagger \mathbf{\Psi}_j|^2 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad K = |\mathbf{\Psi} \mathbf{\Psi}^\dagger|^{\odot 2} \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times M}$$
One BLAS matrix multiplication evaluates the entire kernel matrix in a single parallel operation!
  • Precomputed Statevectors: Compute the 16-element statevector $\mathbf{\Psi} \in \mathbb{C}^{M \times 16}$ once (only 250 simulations instead of 56,125!).
  • Exact Zero-Shot-Noise Fidelity: Yields mathematically exact transition probabilities. The Gram matrix is strictly positive semi-definite.
  • Speedup Outcome: Total training & evaluation time dropped from $> 300\text{s}$ down to 0.42 seconds ($700\times$ faster)!

Critical Analysis & Future Scope

Challenges, NISQ Limitations & Future Scope

Acknowledging hardware hurdles and outlining promising extensions for practical quantum advantage.

Current NISQ Era Limitations

  • Thermal Noise & Decoherence: Superconducting qubits suffer from $T_1$ relaxation and $T_2$ dephasing limits ($\sim 100\mu\text{s}$), causing gate noise in deep circuits.
  • Shot Noise on Real QPUs: Physical hardware cannot inspect statevectors; measuring fidelity requires 10,000+ shots per pair, reintroducing runtime bottlenecks.
  • Finite Feature Capacity: 4 qubits represent 4 technical indicators. Representing hundreds of market signals requires larger quantum registers.

Promising Future Research Horizons

  • Scaling to 20+ Qubits: Incorporating limit order-book depth, options implied volatility surfaces, and macro yield curves in a $2^{20} \approx 10^6$ Hilbert space.
  • Hardware Execution on IBM Eagle: Testing compute-uncompute circuits on real 127-qubit IBM Quantum processors with Zero-Noise Extrapolation (ZNE).
  • Trainable Quantum Kernels: Applying quantum metric learning to optimize rotation parameters $\theta_i$ for maximal kernel alignment with market regimes.

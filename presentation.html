Part 1: Classical Machine Learning

Classical Support Vector Machine: The Maximum Margin Principle

Supervised binary classification grounded in Vapnik-Chervonenkis structural risk minimization.

Geometric Margin & Support Vectors

  • Decision Hyperplane: Separates classes using $w \cdot x + b = 0$.
  • Margin Maximization: Maximizes the geometric margin $\frac{2}{\|w\|}$, reducing overfitting bounds.
  • Support Vectors: Only points lying on boundary margins $y_i(w \cdot x_i + b) = 1$ dictate the decision plane; all interior points can be discarded.
  • Soft-Margin Primal Problem: $$\min_{w, b, \xi} \frac{1}{2}\|w\|^2 + C \sum_{i=1}^M \xi_i \quad \text{s.t.} \quad y_i(w \cdot x_i + b) \ge 1 - \xi_i$$

Lagrangian Dual Formulation

  • Converting primal to dual via Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions: $$\max_{\alpha} \sum_{i=1}^M \alpha_i - \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i,j=1}^M \alpha_i \alpha_j y_i y_j (x_i \cdot x_j)$$ $$\text{s.t.} \quad 0 \le \alpha_i \le C \quad \text{and} \quad \sum_{i=1}^M \alpha_i y_i = 0$$
  • Crucial Property: Data enters strictly via inner products $(x_i \cdot x_j)$!
  • Decision Function: $f(x) = \text{sign}\left( \sum_{i=1}^M \alpha_i y_i (x_i \cdot x) + b \right)$
Part 1: Classical Machine Learning

The Kernel Trick: Overcoming Non-Linearity

Operating implicitly in high-dimensional feature spaces without computing coordinates.

The Kernel Principle

  • Real-world data (such as financial markets) is rarely linearly separable in input space $\mathbb{R}^N$.
  • We map data to a higher-dimensional space $\mathcal{H}$ via non-linear mapping $\phi(x)$.
  • The Trick: We never explicitly compute $\phi(x)$. We replace inner product $\langle \phi(x_i), \phi(x_j) \rangle$ with a kernel function $k(x_i, x_j)$.
  • Mercer's Condition: Any symmetric, positive semi-definite continuous function satisfies: $$k(x, x') = \langle \phi(x), \phi(x') \rangle$$

Standard Classical Kernels & Bottleneck

  • Linear: $k(x, x') = x \cdot x'$
  • Polynomial: $k(x, x') = (x \cdot x' + c)^d$
  • RBF (Gaussian): $k(x, x') = \exp(-\gamma \|x - x'\|^2)$ (maps to infinite-dimensional RKHS based on Euclidean distance).
  • The Classical Bottleneck: Classical kernels are constrained to functions efficiently computable on classical CPUs and treat feature space as local metric balls. Can quantum states unlock richer non-linear correlations?
Part 2: The Quantum Transition

The Quantum Leap: Why Combine Quantum & SVM?

The natural mathematical synergy between quantum state overlaps and kernel methods.

1. Natural Inner Product Evaluator

SVM needs only inner products $k(x_i, x_j)$. Quantum hardware cannot easily output large vectors without tomography, but it natively measures quantum state overlaps (fidelity $|\langle \psi(x_i) | \psi(x_j) \rangle|^2$) through interference!

2. Exponential Hilbert Space

An $n$-qubit register lives in a $2^n$-dimensional Hilbert space. A modest 4-qubit system spans 16 dimensions; 20 qubits span 1,048,576 dimensions; 50 qubits span $\approx 1.1 \times 10^{15}$ dimensions!

3. Classically Intractable Kernels

Havlíček et al. (Nature, 2019) proved that quantum circuits with non-commuting entangling gates generate kernels conjectured to be hard to estimate classically.

4. Guaranteed Convex Optimization

Variational Quantum Classifiers (VQC) suffer from barren plateaus (vanishing gradients). QSVM completely circumvents this: quantum hardware computes the kernel matrix, and a classical QP solver guarantees the global optimum!

Part 2: Quantum Data Encoding

Quantum Feature Maps: Encoding Classical Data into Qubits

Unitary mapping $U_\phi(x)$ transforming feature vector $\vec{x}$ into quantum state $|\psi(x)\rangle$.

Mathematical Formalism

  • Quantum state preparation: $$|\psi(x)\rangle = U_\phi(x) |0\rangle^{\otimes n}$$
  • Quantum kernel as transition probability (fidelity): $$k(x, x') = |\langle \psi(x) | \psi(x') \rangle|^2 = |\langle 0|^{\otimes n} U_\phi^\dagger(x') U_\phi(x) |0\rangle^{\otimes n}|^2$$
  • Properties: Symmetric, strictly bounded in $[0, 1]$, and produces a positive semi-definite Gram matrix $K \ge 0$.
  • $k(x, x) = 1.0$ (identical vectors); $k(x, x') = 0.0$ (orthogonal quantum states).

Second-Order Pauli ZZ Feature Map

  • Circuit form: $U_\phi(x) = \left[ U_Z(x) H^{\otimes n} \right]^t$ ($t=2$ repetitions):
  • 1. Superposition: Hadamard gates $H^{\otimes n}$ place all qubits into equal superposition.
  • 2. Single-Qubit Phase: Encodes individual features: $$\exp\left( i \sum_{i=1}^n x_i Z_i \right)$$
  • 3. Two-Qubit Entanglement: Entangles adjacent pairs based on pairwise products: $$\exp\left( i \sum_{i < j} (\pi - x_i)(\pi - x_j) Z_i Z_j \right)$$
Part 3: QSVM Paradigms

Two Formulations of Quantum SVM in Literature

Comparing theoretical HHL least-squares QSVM with the NISQ-ready hybrid quantum kernel paradigm.

Paradigm 1: HHL-Based Least-Squares QSVM

Rebentrost, Mohseni, Lloyd (PRL, 2014)
  • Recasts Least-Squares SVM as a linear system: $$\begin{bmatrix} 0 & \mathbf{1}^T \\ \mathbf{1} & K + \gamma^{-1} I \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} b \\ \alpha \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ y \end{bmatrix}$$
  • Inverts matrix via quantum phase estimation and HHL algorithm in $O(\log(NM))$ runtime!
  • Practical Obstacle: Requires fault-tolerant hardware, Quantum RAM (QRAM) for data loading, and low condition number $\kappa$.

Paradigm 2: Quantum Kernel Estimation (Our Implementation)

Havlíček et al. (Nature, 2019) / Schuld & Killoran (2019)
  • Hybrid Architecture: Quantum computer computes the Gram matrix $K_{ij}$; classical solver solves the convex dual QP.
  • NISQ Ready: Shallow circuit depth, requires zero QRAM, runs on near-term superconducting QPUs.
  • Convex Global Optimum: Retains standard SVM convergence without barren plateaus.
Part 3: Circuit Architecture

The Compute-Uncompute Kernel Estimation Circuit

Estimating state overlap using inversion testing without auxiliary ancilla qubits.

Compute-Uncompute Inversion Test

|0⟩ ──[ U_φ(x_j) ]──[ U_φ†(x_i) ]──[ ∡ ] ─> c_0 |0⟩ ──[ U_φ(x_j) ]──[ U_φ†(x_i) ]──[ ∡ ] ─> c_1 |0⟩ ──[ U_φ(x_j) ]──[ U_φ†(x_i) ]──[ ∡ ] ─> c_2 |0⟩ ──[ U_φ(x_j) ]──[ U_φ†(x_i) ]──[ ∡ ] ─> c_3
  • Applies unitary $U_\phi(x_j)$ followed by inverse unitary $U_\phi^\dagger(x_i)$ on ground state $|0000\rangle$.
  • If $x_i = x_j$, $U_\phi^\dagger(x_i) U_\phi(x_j) = I$ (returns to $|0000\rangle$ with probability 1.0).
  • The probability of measuring all zeros gives the fidelity: $P(|0\dots0\rangle) = k(x_i, x_j)$.

Advantages over SWAP Test

  • Hardware Efficiency: SWAP test requires $2n + 1$ qubits (two registers + ancilla). Inversion test requires only $n$ qubits!
  • Gate Count: Avoids expensive controlled-SWAP (Fredkin) gates.
  • Sample Shots: Measuring $s$ shots gives empirical frequency $\hat{k}$. Statistical error scales as $O(1/\sqrt{s})$ ($s \approx 10,000$ for $\varepsilon=0.01$).
  • Gram Matrix Construction: Symmetric $M \times M$ matrix requiring $M(M-1)/2$ off-diagonal circuits.
Part 4: Real-World Application

Project Domain: Stock Market Trend Direction Classification

Evaluating whether quantum non-linear kernels can predict daily price trajectory on Indian and US equities.

Binary Classification Formulation

  • Target Objective: Predict next-day closing direction:
    • Class 1 (UP): $\text{Close}_{t+1} > \text{Close}_t$
    • Class 0 (DOWN): $\text{Close}_{t+1} \le \text{Close}_t$
  • Live Data Source: 5 Years of daily OHLCV historical feeds retrieved live via Yahoo Finance (yfinance).
  • Indian Market (NSE): Reliance Industries, TCS, HDFC Bank, SBI, Infosys, ITC, Bharti Airtel, L&T, etc.
  • US Market: Apple, Microsoft, NVIDIA, Google, Amazon, Tesla.

Financial Time Series Reality

  • Low Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR): Financial markets behave largely like Brownian motion random walks.
  • The 50–55% Baseline Reality: Unlike deterministic physics benchmarks, predicting directional market moves with $>55\%$ out-of-sample accuracy is institutional-grade. A naive majority-class model yields $\sim 52\text{--}54\%$.
  • Strict Chronological Split: Shuffled k-fold cross-validation is forbidden due to look-ahead bias. Training strictly precedes testing.
Part 4: Data Preprocessing

Feature Engineering & The Crucial $[0, \pi]$ Quantum Scaling

Extracting 4 technical indicators and mapping them monotonically onto the Bloch sphere.

4 Classical Technical Features

  • 1. RSI (14-day): Relative Strength Index measuring price momentum and overbought/oversold boundaries.
  • 2. MACD Histogram: Difference between (12-day EMA − 26-day EMA) and the 9-day signal EMA line.
  • 3. Moving Average Ratio: $\text{Close} / \text{SMA}(20)$ quantifying deviation from the short-term trend.
  • 4. Daily Volume % Change: Relative change in volume indicating breakout liquidity.

Why Scale Features to $[0, \pi]$?

  • Chronological Split: Most recent 350 trading days: first 250 days for training, last 100 days for testing.
  • Periodic Phase Wrapping: Quantum phase gates rotate states as: $$R_z(\theta) = \begin{bmatrix} e^{-i\theta/2} & 0 \\ 0 & e^{i\theta/2} \end{bmatrix}, \quad R_z(\theta + 2\pi) = R_z(\theta)$$
  • If features are unscaled, extreme values wrap around $2\pi$, causing distinct data points to alias to the exact same quantum state!
  • Scaling strictly to $[0, \pi]$ guarantees monotonic separation on the Bloch sphere with zero phase aliasing.
Part 5: Quantum Implementation

4-Qubit Circuit Architecture for Stock Prediction

Mapping classical indicators into quantum registers with nearest-neighbor linear entanglement.

Qubit Allocation & Hardware Coupling

  • $q_0 \leftrightarrow x_0$: Normalized RSI (14-day)
  • $q_1 \leftrightarrow x_1$: Normalized MACD Histogram
  • $q_2 \leftrightarrow x_2$: Normalized Moving Average Ratio
  • $q_3 \leftrightarrow x_3$: Normalized Daily Volume Change
  • Nearest-Neighbor Linear Coupling:
    • Pair $(q_0, q_1)$: Entangles RSI momentum with MACD divergence.
    • Pair $(q_1, q_2)$: Entangles MACD with trend deviation.
    • Pair $(q_2, q_3)$: Entangles trend deviation with volume.

Circuit Complexity (2 Repetitions)

  • Superposition Layer: 4 Hadamard gates.
  • Single-Qubit Phase: 4 parameterised $P(2x_i)$ gates.
  • Linear ZZ Entanglement:
    • 3 pairs $\times$ [CNOT + $P(2(\pi-x_i)(\pi-x_j))$ + CNOT]
    • = 6 CNOT gates + 3 Phase gates per repetition.
  • Total Gate Count: 16 single-qubit gates + 12 CNOT gates.
  • Hilbert Dimension: $2^4 = 16$-dimensional complex projective space.
Part 5: Engineering Innovation

Simulation Optimization: Achieving a 700× Speedup

Overcoming the 5-minute Qiskit bottleneck with vectorized Hermitian inner products.

The Naive Qiskit Bottleneck

  • Training Gram Matrix: $250 \times 249 / 2 = 31,125$ circuit pairs.
  • Testing Gram Matrix: $100 \times 250 = 25,000$ circuit pairs.
  • Total individual circuits: $>56,000$ circuits!
  • Evaluating 56,000 circuits sequentially in Python took >5 minutes, causing cloud server timeouts and introducing shot noise.

Vectorized Statevector Solution

  • Fidelity is analytically identical to the statevector dot product: $$k(x_i, x_j) = |\langle \psi(x_i) | \psi(x_j) \rangle|^2$$
  • Step 1: Pre-generate all 250 training statevectors once into matrix $\Psi_{\text{train}} \in \mathbb{C}^{250 \times 16}$ (takes 0.3s).
  • Step 2: Compute entire Gram matrix via BLAS matrix multiplication: $$K_{\text{train}} = |\Psi_{\text{train}} \cdot \Psi_{\text{train}}^\dagger|^2 \quad (\text{takes } 0.005\text{s}!)$$
  • Result: Runtime dropped from $>5$ minutes to 0.4 seconds (700× speedup) with zero shot noise!

Critical Evaluation

Challenges, NISQ Limitations & Future Scope

Critical bottlenecks in near-term quantum computing and research directions for quantum advantage.

NISQ Challenges

  • Decoherence & Gate Errors: Two-qubit gate error rates ($\sim 10^{-2}$) accumulate as circuit depth increases.
  • Exponential Kernel Concentration: Thanasilp et al. (2022) proved that as qubit count $n$ grows, quantum kernels concentrate exponentially around a constant, degrading trainability.
  • Shot Noise: Finite sampling introduces $O(1/\sqrt{s})$ statistical variance.

Future Research Horizons

  • Trainable Quantum Kernels: Optimizing parameterized rotation gates via Kernel Alignment with training labels $y_i y_j$.
  • Problem-Informed Inductive Biases: Designing feature maps tailored to cross-asset covariance matrices rather than generic circuits.
  • Fault-Tolerant HHL Execution: Realizing $O(\log(NM))$ runtime when logical qubits and QRAM become viable.
